\section*{I. Data to Be Collected}

\subsection*{a. Goal}

\noindent\textbf{Mission Objective:} REQ.03.01 --- The ground-penetrating radar sounder shall resolve
ice shell thickness to within $\pm$10\% accuracy for ice layers between 1~km and 30~km depth,
producing a subsurface map at a minimum spatial resolution of 100~m along the traverse track.
This requirement is directly traceable to the overarching mission objective of autonomously
identifying and reaching an optimal cryobot insertion site on Europa's surface.

\noindent\textbf{Data Product Goal:} The data product is a georeferenced subsurface ice-thickness
map assembled from continuous ground-penetrating radar (GPR) sounder returns collected
during surface traversal. It provides mission engineers and onboard autonomy with a
spatially resolved picture of where the ice shell is thinnest along the planned route, enabling
data-driven site selection for cryobot deployment without real-time Earth support. Scientists
receive a secondary product --- a dielectric permittivity profile --- that constrains ice
composition and the presence of brine layers that would affect cryobot thermal penetration
rates.

\noindent\textbf{Success Criterion:} The map is considered mission-sufficient when it covers the
full planned traverse track at no worse than 100~m along-track spatial resolution and reports
ice thickness values with a demonstrated accuracy of $\pm$10\% against calibration targets
across the 1--30~km depth range (REQ.03.01). The onboard site-selection algorithm must
be able to ingest this map and autonomously designate a candidate insertion waypoint without
a ground-in-the-loop decision.


\subsection*{b. Data Collected by the Robot}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]

    \item \textbf{Category 1 --- Radar Sounder Returns (Primary Science Data):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Two-way travel time of radar pulse to primary basal reflector (nanoseconds, ns)
        \item Received signal amplitude at basal reflector (dB relative to transmitted power)
        \item Estimated dielectric permittivity of bulk ice column (dimensionless, $\varepsilon_r$)
        \item Derived ice shell thickness from travel-time inversion (meters, m)
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Category 2 --- Rover Localization and Pose:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Rover position estimate from visual odometry and inter-agent ranging (meters, m; in
              local ENU frame)
        \item Position uncertainty (1-$\sigma$ ellipse semi-axes, meters)
        \item Rover heading (degrees, relative to local North)
        \item Accumulated odometry error flag (Boolean: within $\pm$0.5~m bound per REQ.02.01)
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Category 3 --- Surface Terrain Context:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Surface slope at measurement site (degrees)
        \item Terrain roughness index derived from stereo or lidar returns (dimensionless, 0--1 scale)
        \item Surface temperature at measurement site (Kelvin, K)
        \item Estimated surface albedo at measurement site (dimensionless, 0--1 scale)
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Category 4 --- System Health and Data Quality Flags:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Total ionizing dose accumulated by radar front-end electronics at time of measurement
              (krad)
        \item Radar sounder noise floor at time of measurement (dB)
        \item Inter-agent network link quality at time of measurement (packet loss rate, \%)
        \item Data quality flag for each radar trace (enumerated: NOMINAL / CLUTTER /
              NOISE\_FLOOR / INVALID)
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Daily Summary:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Total traverse distance completed in sol (meters, m)
        \item Number of valid radar traces collected in sol (count)
        \item Minimum ice thickness value observed in sol (meters, m) and associated position
        \item Number of candidate insertion sites flagged by onboard site-selection algorithm
              (count)
        \item Mean consensus convergence time for terrain map updates in sol (minutes)
    \end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\section*{II. Types of Measurements on the Spreadsheet}

\noindent The spreadsheet is organized into multiple sheets to keep the dataset clear and usable.
Below is a breakdown of each sheet:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Sheet 1 --- Radar\_Traces:}\\
    One row per radar sounder measurement. Columns capture the along-track position
    (local X, Y coordinates in meters), two-way travel time (ns), received amplitude (dB),
    estimated permittivity $\varepsilon_r$, derived ice thickness (m), and per-trace data
    quality flag. This is the raw science record; every other sheet derives from it.
    Columns are locked after initial population to preserve data integrity. A permittivity
    lookup table embedded in the sheet converts travel time to thickness using the
    formula $d = \frac{c \cdot t}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}}$, expressed as an Excel formula
    referencing the measured travel-time and permittivity columns rather than hardcoded
    values.

    \item \textbf{Sheet 2 --- Localization\_Log:}\\
    One row per odometry update, timestamped and synchronized with radar trace indices
    for georeferencing. Columns include rover position (X, Y, m), heading (deg), 1-$\sigma$
    position uncertainty (m), and the Boolean localization error flag. This sheet provides
    the spatial backbone that maps each radar trace in Sheet~1 to a physical surface
    location. Cross-sheet \texttt{INDEX/MATCH} formulas in Sheet~1 pull the nearest
    position fix for each radar trace timestamp.

    \item \textbf{Sheet 3 --- Terrain\_and\_Health:}\\
    One row per measurement epoch, aligned to the same trace index as Sheet~1. Captures
    surface slope (deg), terrain roughness index, surface temperature (K), accumulated
    TID (krad), radar noise floor (dB), and inter-agent link packet loss (\%). This sheet
    contextualizes each radar measurement: high slope or high roughness values flag
    geometrically challenging measurement conditions that degrade thickness accuracy,
    while elevated TID or noise floor values inform science team confidence in the
    corresponding trace.

    \item \textbf{Sheet 4 --- Daily Summary:}\\
    One row per sol of operations. Auto-populated via Excel formulas referencing Sheets
    1--3: total traverse distance (\texttt{SUM} of incremental odometry steps), valid
    trace count (\texttt{COUNTIF} on quality flag column), minimum observed ice thickness
    (\texttt{MIN} on derived thickness column for the sol), candidate site count
    (\texttt{COUNTIF} on a site-selection flag column), and mean consensus convergence
    time. This sheet is the primary operational report delivered to mission planning each
    sol and feeds directly into the onboard replan trigger logic.

\end{enumerate}

\section*{IV. Rationale and Alignment with Mission Objective}

\noindent\textbf{Alignment with Objective:}
Every data category maps directly to a requirement in the mission requirements document.
The radar sounder returns (Category 1) are the primary data product required by REQ.03.01:
without two-way travel time and permittivity, no thickness inversion is possible and the
cryobot insertion site cannot be selected. The localization log (Category 2) is required
by REQ.02.01: each radar trace must be georeferenced to $\pm$0.5~m to produce a spatially
accurate subsurface map. Without position, the sounder data is a series of unlocated
depth readings rather than a map. Terrain context (Category 3) directly feeds the
autonomous site-selection logic: surface slope and roughness constrain physically
accessible insertion sites independent of ice thickness, and temperature informs thermal
modeling for cryobot descent rate estimation. System health flags (Category 4) are
required by REQ.01.02 and REQ.04.01: elevated TID accumulation can degrade radar
front-end performance, and inter-agent link quality affects the timeliness of map consensus
updates that drive traverse planning.

\noindent\textbf{Why These Measurements Are Sufficient:}
The four categories together provide complete closure on REQ.03.01 and its supporting
requirements. Two-way travel time plus permittivity produces thickness; localization pins
it to a map coordinate; terrain context filters candidate sites by physical accessibility;
health flags bound the confidence of every measurement. The daily summary sheet provides
the aggregate statistics needed by the onboard planner to decide whether to continue
traversing or commit to an insertion attempt. No additional measurements are required
to make the site-selection decision autonomously.

\noindent\textbf{Potential Gaps or Limitations:}
The primary limitation is that permittivity estimation from GPR returns alone carries
uncertainty when the ice column contains brine inclusions or heterogeneous layering,
as identified in RISK-05 of the requirements document. Brine lenses increase local
permittivity and produce spurious reflections that could cause the thickness algorithm
to underestimate depth to the basal interface. The data product mitigates this by flagging
affected traces with the CLUTTER quality code, but it does not eliminate the ambiguity
without a secondary instrument (e.g., a seismic sensor or a multi-frequency radar). A
second limitation is that along-track spatial resolution of 100~m is defined by REQ.03.01
but cross-track coverage depends on the swarm's orbital relay scan width, which is not
yet specified; the current dataset captures a 1D profile along the traverse, not a
2D areal map. Future mission iterations should consider expanding the swarm's orbital
synthetic aperture to provide cross-track coverage before committing to a surface traverse
direction.
