% SECTION 0: LINK DEFINITION
% ====================================================
\section*{I. Link Definition and Desired Margin}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Next communication node:} the nearer of the two orbital relay agents. The rover does
    not run its mission through a direct-to-Earth link; Section~V shows why. Uplink is taken in the
    assignment's sense of rover~$\rightarrow$~relay (the science return direction), and downlink as
    relay~$\rightarrow$~rover (the command forward direction).

    \item \textbf{Band and channel:} UHF proximity link on the CCSDS Proximity-1 return/forward
    pair, \SI{401.6}{MHz} return and \SI{437.1}{MHz} forward, the same split flown by Electra and
    Electra-Lite on the Mars Relay Network.

    \item \textbf{Desired margin:} \SI{3}{dB} above the $E_b/N_0$ required for a post-decoding bit
    error rate of $10^{-6}$. With CCSDS turbo coding at rate $1/2$ that threshold is
    $\approx \SI{1.5}{dB}$, so the link must deliver at least \SI{4.5}{dB}. Three decibels is the
    right allowance here because the relay orbit geometry, the helix pattern at the rover, and the
    Jovian synchrotron contribution to receiver noise are all only coarsely known before flight.

    \item \textbf{Worst-case geometry:} both relays fly a \SI{200}{km} circular Europa orbit. With
    $R_{\mathrm{Europa}} = \SI{1561}{km}$ and a \SI{10}{\degree} elevation mask, the slant range at
    the edge of the pass is
    \[
        R = -R_E \sin\varepsilon + \sqrt{R_E^2\sin^2\varepsilon + \left(r^2 - R_E^2\right)}
          = \SI{588}{km},
    \]
    against \SI{200}{km} at zenith. Every budget below uses the \SI{588}{km} worst case.
\end{enumerate}

% ====================================================
% SECTION II: ASSUMED PARAMETERS
% ====================================================
\section*{II. Assumed Parameters}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
    \item $P_t = \SI{5}{W}$ RF (rover), $\SI{8}{W}$ RF (relay). The rover's UHF radio draws
    \SI{35}{W} DC in the power budget; an Electra-Lite class transceiver puts roughly
    \SI{5}{W} of that on the antenna. The relay agent is not power-constrained the way the rover is,
    so it carries a larger amplifier.

    \item $G_t = 3.16$ (\SI{5.0}{dBi}) rover quadrifilar helix; $G_r = 5.01$ (\SI{7.0}{dBi}) relay
    UHF patch array. The helix is the antenna already carried in the mass budget, and its
    near-hemispherical pattern is what lets the rover close a pass without pointing.

    \item $L_a = 0.89$ ($\SI{-0.51}{dB}$). Europa has no meaningful atmosphere at
    $\sim\!10^{-12}$~bar, so this term is not atmospheric absorption. It covers circular-to-linear
    polarization mismatch and Jovian plasma scintillation on the path.

    \item $L_l = 0.71$ ($\SI{-1.49}{dB}$) transmitter-to-antenna line loss, from cabling and
    connectors run to a mast-mounted antenna and derated for cryogenic operation at \SI{90}{K}.

    \item $\lambda = \SI{0.7465}{m}$ return (\SI{401.6}{MHz}), $\lambda = \SI{0.6859}{m}$ forward
    (\SI{437.1}{MHz}), from $\lambda = c/f$.

    \item $k = \SI{1.38e-23}{J/K}$.

    \item $T_s = \SI{3000}{K}$ at the relay receiver, $\SI{4000}{K}$ at the rover receiver. This is
    the parameter that separates a Europa proximity link from a Mars one. At \SI{400}{MHz} the
    receiver sees galactic background plus Jupiter's synchrotron emission, and the rover's front end
    has no cryogenic cooling and a warmer view of the source, so it is assigned the higher figure.

    \item $R = \SI{5.879e5}{m}$, the \SI{10}{\degree}-elevation slant range derived above.

    \item $R_b = \SI{128}{kbps}$ return, \SI{8}{kbps} forward. These are the floors set by
    REQ.10.01: \SI{55}{MB} of compressed data per sol delivered inside the \SI{90}{min} relay window
    the ConOps allocates works out to \SI{90}{kbps}, and \SI{128}{kbps} is the next standard step up.
\end{enumerate}

% ====================================================
% SECTION III: METHOD
% ====================================================
\section*{III. Method}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
    \item Ratio form:
    \[
        \frac{E_b}{N_0} = \frac{P_t G_t G_r L_a L_l \lambda^2}{(4\pi R)^2\, k\, T_s\, R_b}
    \]
    \item Decibel form: $\left(E_b/N_0\right)_{\mathrm{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\left(E_b/N_0\right)$
    \item Margin: $M = \left(E_b/N_0\right)_{\mathrm{dB}} - \left(E_b/N_0\right)_{\mathrm{req}}$,
    with $\left(E_b/N_0\right)_{\mathrm{req}} = \SI{1.5}{dB}$.
    \item The link closes when $M \geq \SI{3}{dB}$.
\end{enumerate}

% ====================================================
% SECTION IV: UPLINK
% ====================================================
\section*{IV. Uplink --- Rover to Relay (Science Return)}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
    \item Numerator:
    \[
        P_t G_t G_r L_a L_l \lambda^2
        = 5 \times 3.16 \times 5.01 \times 0.89 \times 0.71 \times (0.7465)^2
        = 27.87
    \]
    \item Denominator:
    \[
        (4\pi R)^2 k T_s R_b
        = \left(4\pi \times 5.879\!\times\!10^5\right)^2 \times \SI{1.38e-23}{} \times 3000
          \times 1.28\!\times\!10^5
        = 0.2892
    \]
    \item Result: $E_b/N_0 = 27.87 / 0.2892 = 96.4$
    \item In decibels: $10\log_{10}(96.4) = \SI{19.84}{dB}$
    \item Margin: $19.84 - 1.5 = \SI{18.3}{dB}$. \textbf{The uplink closes.}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Term-by-term, in decibels, for traceability:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Term & Value (dB) \\
\midrule
$P_t$ (\SI{5}{W})                        & $+6.99$ \\
$G_t$ (rover helix)                      & $+5.00$ \\
$G_r$ (relay patch array)                & $+7.00$ \\
$L_a$ (polarization + plasma)            & $-0.51$ \\
$L_l$ (line loss)                        & $-1.49$ \\
$\lambda^2$                              & $-2.54$ \\
$(4\pi R)^2$ (space loss at \SI{588}{km})& $-137.37$ \\

$T_s$ (\SI{3000}{K})                     & $-34.77$ \\
$R_b$ (\SI{128}{kbps})                   & $-51.07$ \\
\midrule
$E_b/N_0$                                & $\mathbf{+19.84}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

% ====================================================
% SECTION V: DOWNLINK
% ====================================================
\section*{V. Downlink --- Relay to Rover (Command Forward)}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
    \item Numerator:
    \[
        8 \times 5.01 \times 3.16 \times 0.89 \times 0.71 \times (0.6859)^2 = 37.65
    \]
    \item Denominator:
    \[
        \left(4\pi \times 5.879\!\times\!10^5\right)^2 \times \SI{1.38e-23}{} \times 4000
          \times 8.0\!\times\!10^3 = 0.02410
    \]
    \item Result: $E_b/N_0 = 37.65 / 0.02410 = 1562$
    \item In decibels: $10\log_{10}(1562) = \SI{31.94}{dB}$
    \item Margin: $31.94 - 1.5 = \SI{30.4}{dB}$. \textbf{The downlink closes.}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The forward direction has an easier job: the relay transmits more power from a higher-gain
antenna, and the command load is \SI{8}{kbps} against \SI{128}{kbps} of science return. The
\SI{16}{\times} rate difference alone accounts for \SI{12}{dB} of the gap.

% ====================================================
% SECTION VI: DIRECT-TO-EARTH CHECK
% ====================================================
\section*{VI. Direct-to-Earth Check (Why the Relay Exists)}

The same equation run on the rover's X-band low-gain antenna against a DSN \SI{34}{m} BWG station
sets the fallback rate. Using $P_t = \SI{15}{W}$, $G_t = 6.31$ (\SI{8}{dBi} LGA),
$G_r = 6.6\!\times\!10^6$ (\SI{68.2}{dBi}), $\lambda = \SI{0.0357}{m}$ (\SI{8.4}{GHz}),
$T_s = \SI{30}{K}$ (cryogenic DSN front end), and solving for the rate that still holds
\SI{4.5}{dB}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Earth range & $R$ (m) & Max rate at \SI{4.5}{dB} \\
\midrule
Opposition ($\SI{4.2}{AU}$)  & $6.28\!\times\!10^{11}$ & \SI{6.9}{bps} \\
Conjunction ($\SI{6.2}{AU}$) & $9.27\!\times\!10^{11}$ & \SI{3.2}{bps} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Seven bits per second is a state-of-health beacon, not a science link: one \SI{55}{MB} sol
of REQ.10.01 would take just over two years to clear. This is the quantitative reason the relay agents are
architectural rather than optional, and it is the number REQ.10.03 fixes as the \SI{5}{bps}
fallback floor.

% ====================================================
% SECTION VII: REFLECTION
% ====================================================
\section*{VII. Reflection}

Both directions close, and by a wide margin: \SI{18.3}{dB} on the return and \SI{30.4}{dB} on the
forward. The interesting result is that the proximity link is not power-limited at all. At
\SI{4.5}{dB} the return would support roughly \SI{4.4}{Mbps}, so the real ceiling is the radio's
\SI{2048}{kbps} maximum and the \SI{90}{min} pass, not the RF budget. That surplus is worth keeping
rather than spending, since $T_s$ is the weakest assumption I made and Jupiter's synchrotron noise
at \SI{400}{MHz} could plausibly be several times my \SI{3000}{K}. If the link did fail, the first
parameter I would change is $R_b$, because it is free, and the second is $G_r$ at the relay, since
adding patch elements to an orbiter is cheaper in mass and power than anything on the rover.
